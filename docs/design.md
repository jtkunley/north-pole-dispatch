# North Pole Dispatch
### High-Level System Design

*An event-driven, self-regulating gift fulfillment engine built in Go.*

**Project Codename:** SleighOps  
**Project Type:** Backend Engineering Reference Implementation  
**Language:** Go

---

## 1. Overview

North Pole Dispatch is an event-driven simulation of Santa's gift fulfillment operation at the North Pole.

The system continuously receives children's Christmas wish lists at fluctuating rates and autonomously coordinates gift inventory, elf workforce capacity, and gift fulfillment.

As demand increases, the workshop automatically replenishes gift inventory and activates additional elves. As demand decreases, inventory replenishment slows and underutilized elves are returned to the available workforce pool.

The system operates continuously, responding to changes through events without requiring manual intervention.

The primary purpose is to demonstrate a concurrent, event-driven backend architecture with transactional consistency, state-machine-driven lifecycles, location-aware resource matching, and adaptive capacity management.

North Pole Dispatch is a self-contained reference implementation designed to run locally without external infrastructure.

## 2. Design Goals

- **Event-Driven Architecture:** System components react to events rather than relying on continuous polling or synchronous orchestration.
- **Transactional Consistency:** Concurrent operations preserve data integrity, preventing duplicate assignments, conflicting state transitions, and inventory inconsistencies.
- **State-Machine Lifecycles:** Wish lists, fulfillment tasks, and elves follow explicitly defined lifecycle transitions.
- **Autonomous Adaptation:** Gift inventory and elf workforce capacity automatically respond to changing demand.
- **Continuous Operation:** The system continues making progress through fluctuations in demand, temporary inventory shortages, and resource constraints.
- **Location-Aware Matching:** Tasks are assigned to available elves based on priority and proximity to their pickup locations.
- **Observability:** Throughput, resource utilization, system health, and recovery behavior are measurable.
- **Simplicity:** Demonstrate advanced backend engineering concepts without unnecessary business or infrastructure complexity.

## 3. Domain Model

The North Pole operates a single workshop containing gift storage shelves, a toy production station, and a gift wrapping station.

The primary domain entities are:

| Entity | Description |
|---|---|
| Workshop | The physical environment containing storage locations, production, and wrapping stations |
| Gift | A catalog item that can be requested on a wish list |
| Inventory | Tracks available, reserved, and incoming gift quantities |
| Wish List | A child's request containing one or more gifts |
| Task | A gift collection or inventory restocking operation |
| Elf | A reusable worker capable of executing one task at a time |

Each gift type has an assigned storage location within the workshop.

Elves have physical locations represented by workshop coordinates. These locations allow the matching engine to estimate travel distance and select nearby elves for available tasks.

A wish list must be fulfilled in its entirety. If one or more requested gifts are unavailable, the wish list waits until sufficient inventory is available to reserve all requested items.

The system supports two types of tasks:

- **Gift Collection:** An elf retrieves a gift from storage and delivers it to the wrapping station.
- **Gift Restocking:** An elf transports newly produced gifts from the production station to their assigned storage location.

Both task types share the same matching and execution infrastructure.

## 4. High-Level Components

### 4.1 Wish List Generator

The Wish List Generator is the single external demand driver for the simulation.

It continuously generates randomized wish lists at a dynamically changing rate, measured in requests per second (RPS).

The arrival rate follows a bounded random walk:

- Configurable minimum and maximum RPS.
- Configurable maximum rate change per tick.
- Random upward or downward rate adjustments.
- Seeded randomness for reproducible simulations.

Each wish list contains a randomized selection of gifts from a small, predefined catalog.

The generator creates natural fluctuations in demand, including periods of low activity, increasing traffic, and sustained peaks.

All other system components respond to the workload generated by this component.

### 4.2 Wish List Fulfillment Coordinator

The Fulfillment Coordinator manages wish lists throughout their lifecycles.

Its responsibilities include:

- Receiving newly submitted wish lists.
- Checking gift inventory availability.
- Reserving all required gifts transactionally.
- Maintaining wish lists that are waiting for inventory.
- Generating gift collection tasks when a wish list becomes eligible.
- Tracking task completions.
- Marking wish lists fulfilled when all requested gifts reach the wrapping station.

Incomplete inventory availability does not prevent unrelated wish lists from progressing.

### 4.3 Gift Inventory Controller

The Inventory Controller maintains gift stock levels and automatically coordinates replenishment.

Inventory requirements adapt to observed demand for each gift type.

Its responsibilities include:

- Tracking available, reserved, and incoming inventory.
- Monitoring demand for individual gifts.
- Adjusting desired inventory levels based on demand.
- Requesting replenishment before stock becomes critically low.
- Accounting for outstanding replenishment requests.
- Notifying the fulfillment system when inventory becomes available.

The workshop assumes an unlimited external supply of gifts.

However, replenishment is not instantaneous. New gifts become available only after production and restocking have completed.

### 4.4 Toy Production Simulator

The Toy Production Simulator represents the external supply of new gifts.

When replenishment is requested, production is scheduled with a randomized completion delay.

Once production finishes, the gifts arrive at the workshop's production station and generate restocking tasks.

This introduces realistic variability in inventory availability without requiring a full manufacturing or supply-chain system.

### 4.5 Elf Workforce Controller

The Elf Workforce Controller manages the number of active elves operating inside the workshop.

It uses configurable rules to adjust workforce capacity according to utilization.

When elf utilization exceeds the configured upper boundary, additional elves are activated.

When utilization falls below the lower boundary, idle elves may be returned to the inactive pool.

The desired operating utilization is derived from the midpoint of the configured boundaries.

The workforce is conceptually elastic, with an unlimited external pool of elves available for activation.

The controller operates independently of gift inventory management and task matching.

### 4.6 Elf Matching Engine

The Elf Matching Engine assigns available tasks to eligible elves.

It considers:

- Task priority.
- Task waiting time.
- Elf availability.
- Elf location and estimated travel time.

The engine prioritizes work according to its scheduling rules and selects an appropriate available elf.

Assignments are performed transactionally to ensure that:

- An elf cannot execute multiple tasks simultaneously.
- A task cannot be assigned to multiple elves.
- Concurrent assignment attempts cannot produce conflicting state changes.

The matching engine is responsible for determining which elf should perform which task, not for managing inventory or workforce scaling policies.

### 4.7 Elf Execution Simulator

The Execution Simulator represents elves physically performing their assignments.

It estimates execution duration using workshop locations, travel distances, and handling time.

For gift collection, an elf travels to a storage location, collects the requested gift, and delivers it to the wrapping station.

For restocking, an elf collects produced gifts and transports them to their assigned storage location.

When execution completes, the simulator generates completion events and returns the elf to the available workforce.

An elf's final location becomes its starting location for subsequent assignments.

Physical collision avoidance and detailed navigation are outside the MVP scope.

### 4.8 Event Runtime

The Event Runtime coordinates event publication, delivery, and processing across the system.

Events represent changes in business state, resource availability, and execution progress.

Examples include:

- `WishListSubmitted`
- `InventoryReplenishmentRequested`
- `GiftsProduced`
- `InventoryRestocked`
- `TaskQueued`
- `TaskAssigned`
- `TaskCompleted`
- `ElfAvailable`
- `WorkforceScaled`
- `WishListFulfilled`

Components respond to relevant events and perform their responsibilities independently.

State changes are committed transactionally, and subsequent processing is driven by the resulting events.

The event runtime supports concurrent processing while preserving system consistency.

### 4.9 Transactional State Store

The Transactional State Store maintains authoritative system state.

It manages:

- Wish lists and their fulfillment status.
- Gift inventory and reservations.
- Tasks and assignments.
- Elf availability and locations.
- State-machine transitions.

Operations that modify related entities must preserve transactional consistency.

The store prevents conflicting updates, invalid state transitions, duplicate task execution, and incorrect inventory allocation.

It provides the foundation for safely increasing concurrent processing capacity.

### 4.10 Observability

The Observability component collects operational and performance metrics from the running system.

Key metrics include:

- Incoming wish list RPS.
- Fulfilled wish list RPS.
- Pending and completed wish lists.
- Active elf count.
- Elf utilization.
- Queued and completed tasks.
- Gift inventory availability.
- Inventory shortages and replenishment activity.
- Assignment and fulfillment latency.
- Invalid or conflicting state-transition attempts.

These metrics will support a dashboard showing how the North Pole responds to fluctuating demand and automatically recovers from temporary resource constraints.

## 5. System Workflow

The system operates as a continuous event-driven cycle:

1. The Wish List Generator submits randomized wish lists at a fluctuating RPS.
2. The Fulfillment Coordinator checks gift inventory.
3. Wish lists with sufficient inventory reserve their gifts and generate collection tasks.
4. Wish lists lacking inventory remain pending.
5. The Inventory Controller monitors demand and requests additional gifts as needed.
6. The Toy Production Simulator produces gifts after randomized delays.
7. Newly produced gifts generate restocking tasks.
8. The Elf Matching Engine assigns collection and restocking tasks to available elves.
9. The Execution Simulator completes assignments and emits completion events.
10. Completion events update inventory, wish list progress, and elf availability.
11. The Workforce Controller adjusts the active elf population based on utilization.
12. The system continues processing incoming wish lists and reacting to internal events.

The entire workflow repeats continuously without requiring manual orchestration.

## 6. State Machines and Transactional Integrity

Three primary entities use explicit state-machine lifecycles.

**Wish List**

Received → Waiting for Inventory / Fulfilling → Fulfilled

**Task**

Queued → Assigned → In Progress → Completed

**Elf**

Inactive → Available → Busy → Available

State transitions are validated and coordinated through transactional operations.

The system must preserve fundamental invariants, including:

- Every wish list is fulfilled at most once.
- A wish list is fulfilled only after all required gifts have been collected.
- Inventory cannot be reserved beyond available quantities.
- Every elf executes at most one task at a time.
- Every task has at most one active assignment.
- Duplicate events cannot cause duplicate business operations.
- Invalid state transitions cannot corrupt system state.

Detailed transition rules and transactional boundaries will be specified in the subsequent domain design.

## 7. Autonomous Operation and Resilience

North Pole Dispatch is designed to be a self-regulating system.

Changes in incoming demand influence both inventory requirements and elf workforce utilization.

The system must tolerate temporary resource shortages without requiring manual intervention.

Examples include:

- Increased wish list traffic causing additional elves to become active.
- Increased demand for specific gifts triggering replenishment.
- Random production delays temporarily preventing wish list fulfillment.
- Newly restocked gifts allowing previously blocked wish lists to progress.
- Declining demand causing excess elves to become inactive.

Temporary shortages may increase queues and fulfillment latency, but unrelated eligible work should continue progressing.

Under sustainable workloads, the system should recover from demand spikes as additional resources become available.

The system is not expected to guarantee zero waiting time or unlimited processing throughput. Its resilience is demonstrated through automatic recovery, continued progress, and consistency under concurrent execution.

## 8. Scope and Constraints

The MVP is intentionally limited to:

- A single North Pole workshop.
- A small, configurable gift catalog.
- Randomized, continuously fluctuating wish list traffic.
- Two task types: gift collection and restocking.
- Identical elves with location-aware assignments.
- Demand-responsive inventory replenishment.
- Elastic elf workforce capacity.
- Event-driven processing.
- Transactional state management.
- Automated simulation and operational metrics.

The following are outside the MVP scope:

- Multiple workshops or geographically distributed deployments.
- Multiple elf specializations.
- Gift manufacturing logistics.
- Detailed packaging and delivery logistics.
- Physical collisions or robotic pathfinding.
- Complex inventory placement optimization.
- External message brokers or cloud infrastructure.
- Production-grade warehouse management features.

The backend should remain self-contained and executable locally.

## 9. Expected Outcome

The completed North Pole Dispatch backend will continuously process randomized Christmas wish lists while autonomously managing gift inventory and elf workforce capacity.

The implementation will demonstrate:

1. **Event-driven coordination** across independently responsible components.
2. **Transactional correctness** under concurrent state changes.
3. **State-machine integrity** across interconnected business lifecycles.
4. **Resource matching** using task priority and spatial proximity.
5. **Adaptive capacity management** driven by fluctuating demand.
6. **Automatic recovery** from temporary inventory and workforce constraints.
7. **Observability** through measurable system behavior and performance metrics.

The simulation and its dashboard will illustrate how the North Pole responds to increasing demand, temporary shortages, and subsequent recovery.

The project's primary engineering value is not the complexity of its warehouse simulation, but its demonstration of a resilient, concurrent, event-driven backend system that continuously adapts while preserving data integrity.
